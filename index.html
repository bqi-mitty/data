How the Data Is Dated

/ 01

Point-in-time availability

For tables under active monitoring, timestamps are designed to reflect true disclosure timing. Where records required manual reconstruction — for example after a collection gap — timestamps reflect when the figure entered our database instead. Table-level detail is documented for each dataset.

/ 02

Documented fallback logic

When disclosure timing data is missing or inconsistent, we fall back to statutory filing deadlines. The full rule set is documented, not hidden.

/ 03

Forward-adjusted, stable pricing

Adjusted close is anchored to the earliest trading date in each dataset and carried forward through dividend events. Once published, a historical adjusted close does not get silently rewritten by a later corporate action — return calculations between any two dates are unaffected either way. The raw, unadjusted close_price is always included alongside it.

Structured, cross-referenced, and timestamped through our point-in-time capture pipeline. Data may contain errors, gaps, or delays. Provided as-is, for research and reference purposes.

FAQ

What is point-in-time data, and why does it matter?
Point-in-time data reflects only the information that was actually available as of each historical date — for example, market cap is calculated using the share capital figure known at that time, not the figure known today. This avoids look-ahead bias in backtests.
What markets and time ranges do you cover?
Taiwan (TWSE), with additional Japan and US datasets available. See the Market Coverage section above for exact date ranges per data type.
What's the difference between Lite and Standard?
Lite includes daily OHLCV only. Standard adds point-in-time market cap, cash and stock dividends, and forward-adjusted close.
Why doesn't adjusted_close match today's actual closing price?
Our adjusted_close is anchored to the earliest date in your dataset and carried forward through each dividend event, rather than being recalculated backward from today's price. This means the historical value you download today won't be silently rewritten by a future corporate action — useful if you need reproducible backtests. Use close_price for the actual traded price, and adjusted_close for return calculations.
How is the data delivered?
By email as a CSV file, typically delivered daily within an 18:00–23:00 (UTC+8) window. No API key or integration required.
